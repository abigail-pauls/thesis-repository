# Responses of soil nitrogen and organic carbon content in leptosol soils to precipitation and temperature variables across seven ecosystems
Abigail Pauls - Haverford College Department of Biology - Senior Thesis

## Abstract:

In the past half century, weather has become more unpredictable, extreme, and intense as a result of climate change, and this trend is expected to continue. While models are able to predict what climatic changes will occur, understanding how these changes will affect ecosystems around the world is more complicated. Previous regional work has found that ecological responses to the same climatic changes vary widely across different biomes. Using existing datasets, this study found that soil nitrogen content decreases as the number of dry days in the year prior to soil measurement increases, suggesting that soil nitrogen is more sensitive to changes in amount of precipitation than intensity. Minimum daily temperature variables also showed a significant negative relationship with soil nitrogen content while there was no statistically significant relationship with maximum daily temperature indicating that increases in minimum temperatures have a stronger effect on soil nitrogen content than the same increases in maximum temperatures. Organic carbon decreases as the number of extremely high precipitation events during the year prior to sampling increases, but there is no significant variation in this trend across biomes. Trends of nitrogen response to dry days and minimum daily temperature variables change across biomes, though no clear patterns emerged. Soil nitrogen and organic carbon content in leptosol soils varies significantly across seven biomes, as does all investigated soil properties, including sand, silt, clay, and coarse fragment content, calcium carbonate equivalents, and soil moisture. In addition to these preliminary findings, this study produced a method of data analysis using publicly available data to investigate climate effects on soils without the need for resource intensive studies. Understanding how soil nutrients respond to climatic change allows for better predictions of the impacts of climate change across the world.
